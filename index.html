About

Shiyan Yang is an Associate Researcher at the Eco-environment Protection Research Institute, Shanghai Academy of Agricultural Sciences. Her research focuses on soil health and risk assessment, and the source–sink processes and risk control of soil pollution. She graduated from Sichuan Agricultural University in 2017 and earned her Ph.D. in Soil Science from Zhejiang University in 2022 under the supervision of Professors Jianming Xu and Xingmei Liu. Supported by the China Scholarship Council, she spent one year in joint doctoral training at the Department of Geography, National University of Singapore, before joining the Academy in July 2022. Her work integrates multisource data, probabilistic risk assessment, machine learning and spatial analysis to investigate the spatiotemporal dynamics, cross-media transport and human exposure pathways of heavy metals in agricultural systems. She developed a bioavailability-based cadmium risk assessment model spanning the source–atmosphere–soil–rice–human continuum, supporting regional risk identification, safety threshold derivation and the safe use of contaminated farmland. She has published more than 30 academic papers, including first-author work in Environment International, Journal of Hazardous Materials and Environmental Pollution, and has led projects funded by the NSFC Young Scientists Fund, Shanghai Pujiang Talent Program and Shanghai Sailing talent development program. Her honors include the Shanghai Magnolia Talent Plan (Pujiang Program) and the Minhang Chunshen Pyramid Talent Program (C2 category). Her recent research outputs include Kunling Zhirang, a multi-agent collaborative system for diagnosing urban farmland soil health.

Research Interests

Soil health and risk assessment

Investigating probabilistic health risks, bioaccessibility and regional variation along the soil–crop–human exposure pathway; developing multiscale assessment methods and evaluating the protective capacity of soil and food contaminant limits.

Source–sink processes and risk control of soil pollution

Integrating source apportionment, machine learning and spatial analysis to identify pollution drivers, transport pathways and risk hotspots; studying contaminant stabilization and biochar-based remediation to support the safe use of contaminated farmland.

Research Contributions

Cross-media transport and full-chain risk assessment. Linking pollution sources and environmental transport to human exposure, with bioavailability incorporated into a source–atmosphere–soil–rice–human risk continuum to connect contaminant fate with health risks.

Multisource pollution tracing and spatial diagnosis. Combining machine learning, spatial bivariate analysis and source apportionment to identify pollution drivers, spatial patterns and risk hotspots in agricultural soils across China and in representative regions.

Bioavailability and safety threshold derivation. Incorporating contaminant speciation, bioaccessibility and multiscale risks into soil cadmium threshold derivation; evaluating the protection afforded by existing limits and investigating cadmium–selenium enrichment and bioavailability in areas with high geogenic backgrounds.
